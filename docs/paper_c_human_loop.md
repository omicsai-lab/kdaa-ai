# Paper C scope: human calibration and empirical validation

Paper C is intentionally **not a prerequisite** for the first repository or Paper B.

## Core research questions

1. Do focal units confirm, narrow, split, or reject AI-generated asset hypotheses?
2. Does KDAA surface credible assets that are missing from conventional profiles or from the focal unit's initial self-description?
3. How does calibration differ by asset tacitness, collectivity, attribution ambiguity, and evidence coverage?
4. Are KDAA opportunities perceived as more specific, feasible, and valuable than generic LLM recommendations?
5. Which opportunities are selected and implemented?
6. What value is realized, what value is captured, and by whom?
7. Does outcome feedback improve later discovery and opportunity matching?

## Potential study sequence

### Study 1: calibration reliability and correction burden

- units: researchers, project teams, or laboratories;
- input: public and voluntarily supplied evidence;
- task: confirm, narrow, split, reject, or defer each asset;
- outcomes: acceptance, boundary changes, attribution corrections, missing assets, time burden;
- design: blinded comparison with conventional profile/LLM baseline when feasible.

### Study 2: opportunity usefulness

- compare KDAA opportunities with generic recommendations;
- rate specificity, novelty, feasibility, evidence basis, strategic fit, and risk;
- record whether the recommendation was already known to the unit.

### Study 3: prospective mobilization

- units select one opportunity;
- pre-register baseline, human and AI tasks, verification gates, and expected gain;
- observe artifact completion, effort, quality, reuse, reach, and unintended effects.

### Study 4: longitudinal value and learning

- follow outcomes over six to eighteen months;
- distinguish realized from captured value;
- update asset maps using successes and failures;
- measure false-positive recurrence and portfolio reallocation.

## Sampling

The theoretical focal unit is general, but the first empirical domain should be bounded. Researchers and research groups are strategically coherent with the existing theory manuscript, repo, and the author's scientific context.

Possible sampling frames:

- biomedical researchers;
- computational laboratories;
- interdisciplinary project teams;
- research-support professionals;
- later, knowledge-intensive firms or professional service units.

## Measurement priorities

Human studies should avoid asking only whether outputs “look useful.” At minimum, collect:

- claim correctness;
- claim boundary quality;
- attribution correctness;
- evidence sufficiency;
- novelty relative to prior awareness;
- actionability;
- perceived surveillance and legitimacy;
- correction time;
- selected opportunity;
- observed implementation and value outcomes.

## Ethics and governance

Research with real individuals or units may require institutional review, consent, data-use agreements, secure storage, and a correction/withdrawal process. Public records do not eliminate these concerns because the system creates new inferences.

## Candidate outlets

The eventual outlet depends on the design and contribution:

- Information & Management for a substantial human/organizational empirical study;
- European Journal of Information Systems or Journal of the Association for Information Systems for strong IS theory and design implications;
- Information and Organization for deep sociotechnical questions of visibility, ownership, surveillance, and value capture;
- Decision Support Systems if the empirical focus is decision quality and transparent recommendation support.

No outlet should be chosen until the actual data and contribution are visible.
